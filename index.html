TL;DR: We introduce CoBPE, a compositional tokenizer that packages lexical tokens with nearby function words and punctuation, representing the same text with 30% fewer sequence positions than BPE. Models trained from scratch with CoBPE at 780M and 1.3B parameters outperform BPE by 1.3 points across a 30-task downstream suite under matched training compute, while the shorter sequences also translate into faster training and inference.

The idea

Standard BPE represents function words, punctuation, capitalization, and lexical content along the same one-dimensional token sequence. Even when these elements form a predictable local pattern, each consumes a separate position and incurs another model step.

CoBPE factors this local structure into reusable modifiers attached to lexical base tokens. In the example above, prepositions, determiners, capitalization, spacing, and punctuation are represented alongside their neighboring lexical bases, reducing the sequence from 18 positions to 9 without discarding the original text.

Method overview

At input, modifier embeddings are added to the base embedding. At output, the model predicts a lexical base followed by modifier values conditioned on that base.

Main findings

30%

Fewer sequence positions

Across both model scales, CoBPE represents the same text with 30% fewer positions than standard BPE. The compression comes from composing frequent grammatical markers and surface variation with lexical bases.

+1.2 / +1.3

Better performance at matched training compute

CoBPE improves the average score on a 30-task evaluation suite by 1.2 points at 780M parameters and 1.3 points at 1.3B parameters, while keeping the architecture, training recipe, and token budget fixed.

1.28×

Compression translates into end-to-end speed

In controlled generation experiments, fewer decoding steps yield a 1.28× inference speedup. In the nanochat speedrun setting, CoBPE also reaches the target capability in 30.4% less training time.

From the paper

Paper abstract

Language models process and generate text sequentially in token units, and the tokenizer determines how much text each inference step covers. Under standard tokenization, a short English phrase such as “On the table.” is usually produced as four separate predictions for the preposition (On), article (the), noun (table), and punctuation (.), where each consumes a sequence position and incurs inference costs.

We introduce CoBPE, a compositional tokenization approach that represents such phrases as a lexical base token (table) attached with a small set of reusable surface modifiers, composed in embedding space at input and predicted jointly at output. In controlled pretraining from scratch at 780M and 1.3B scales, CoBPE shortens sequences by 30% and improves downstream performance by 1.2 points relative to standard BPE under matched training compute.

Our results suggest that part of what is now expressed through token sequences can instead be modeled through structured representations, opening a broad design space for more token-efficient and capable language models.
